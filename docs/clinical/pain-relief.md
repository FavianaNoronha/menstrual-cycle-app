# Period-pain relief content

Shown on "My cramps are bad" (app) and "pain help" (WhatsApp). Warning signs always come first.
**Never include medicine names with doses.** Point to the pack label or a pharmacist.

| Order | Option | Evidence | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Heat on the lower belly (hot-water bag, heat pad) | Strong: reduces pain; similar relief to NSAIDs with fewer side effects | Yuan et al. 2026, Front Med — 57 RCTs, 5,359 participants |
| 2 | Over-the-counter painkiller (NSAID) per pack / pharmacist | Strong: first-line for primary dysmenorrhoea unless contraindicated; ~18% get little relief | NICE CKS; Yuan et al. 2026 |
| 3 | Gentle movement — walking, stretching, yoga | Moderate: largest effect among self-care options, but trials vary in quality | Armour et al. 2019, BMC Complement Altern Med — 23 trials |
| 4 | TENS (high frequency) | Moderate | NICE CKS |
| 5 | Ginger | Some: most consistent among supplements | Review of 21 systematic reviews (doi:10.1002/rfc2.70087) |

Pharmacist prompt (all languages): "Ask which painkiller is safe for you, especially if you have asthma, stomach ulcers, kidney problems, or could be pregnant."

When to see a doctor (non-urgent): pain stops daily life most months; painkillers don't help after 3 cycles; pain started years after periods began or is getting worse; pain with sex, bowel movements or urination; pain outside periods.
