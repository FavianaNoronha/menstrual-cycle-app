# Interview script: 25 women, 30 minutes each

**Goal:** find out whether pain-first help, in her language, on WhatsApp, is something she would actually use, and what makes her stop. We are testing the idea, not selling it.

**Who (aim for a spread):** 25 women aged 18+. At least 8 in tier-2/3 towns or villages, 8 with painful or irregular periods, 5 who have used Flo/Clue/Period Tracker and stopped, 4 who never used any app. Mix of Hindi, Kannada, Telugu, Tamil, English. Interview in her language. Do not record without her OK. Give her a small thank-you (₹200 UPI or equivalent). Never ask for her name in the notes; use P01-P25.

**Ground rules for the interviewer:** ask about what she did last time, not what she would do. Do not pitch. Do not lead ("would you like..."). Silence is fine. If she gets upset, stop and offer to end. Do not give medical advice. Do not record anything she tells you about her health in a shared document with her name.

## Part 1: Her last bad period (8 min)
1. Tell me about your last period. What was the hardest day?
2. Did it stop you from doing anything: school, work, going out, sleeping? Tell me about that day.
3. What did you do about it? What did you take, try, or ask someone? Who did you ask?
4. What would you have wanted at that moment?
5. Have you ever seen a doctor about it? What happened? If not, why not?

## Part 2: Tracking today (6 min)
6. Do you track your period now? How (app, calendar, memory, nothing)?
7. If an app: which one, and what do you not like about it? Have you ever deleted one? Why?
8. Was there ever a time a date or prediction was wrong? What did you do?
9. Who else can see your phone? Has an app or notification ever embarrassed you or caused a problem?

## Part 3: Show and watch (10 min)
Show the WhatsApp bot on a phone (or the sample chat). Let her try it herself in her language.
10. [Task] "Tell it your period started today and that you have bad cramps." Observe: did she get stuck? Did the Saved-line read-back make sense?
11. [Task] "Ask what to do about the pain." Read PLAN together. Ask: what is the first thing you would do? Anything confusing or missing?
12. Show the heads-up message (the "weather forecast" one). What do you think it means? Would you be comfortable getting it on your phone? Where would you not?
13. Would you trust it with this information? What would make you trust it more or less?

## Part 4: Close (6 min)
14. What is the one thing that would make you use this every month?
15. What would make you stop?
16. Would you pay anything for it? For what? (Do not suggest a number. Note what she says.)
17. Who else should we talk to?

## Note-taking sheet (per interview)
P-id | town type | language | pain stopped her day (Y/N) | saw doctor (Y/N) | finished tasks 10 and 11 unaided (Y/N) | understood the heads-up (Y/N) | comfortable on own phone (Y/N) | would use monthly (Y/N/maybe) | top quote (no names)

## Pass / fail: decide BEFORE the interviews
We continue pain-first on WhatsApp only if, out of 25:
- **≥ 15** say the pain stopped their day in the last 3 months (the problem is real and common in this group).
- **≥ 18** finish tasks 10 and 11 without help (the product is usable).
- **≥ 16** understand the heads-up message correctly and **≥ 15** say they'd be comfortable receiving it (the coded wording works).
- **≥ 13** say they would use it monthly ("maybe" does not count).
- **Fewer than 5** raise a safety or trust concern we cannot fix with wording.

If usability passes but monthly use fails, the problem is value, not design: go back to Part 1 and Part 4 answers before building more. If the heads-up is misread by more than 5, rewrite it in that language first. Write down the numbers before looking at quotes.
